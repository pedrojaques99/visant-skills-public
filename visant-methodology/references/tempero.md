# Tempero: every brand has its own seasoning

Tempero is a creative direction, not a checklist. Before you make something for a brand, ask what only this brand has. Then let that material shape your choices: the effects, the transitions, the texture, the rhythm, the voice. A piece seasoned this way feels like the brand. A piece built from presets feels like a template.

*Tempero* means seasoning. It is the house word; `brand idiom` means the same thing. Use judgment: season where it adds something, and hold back where clarity matters more.

## Find the ingredients

Before you start, spend a few minutes looking at the brand's own material. Note what stands out, usually 3 to 6 things, and where each one lives. A short list in the brief or in a `TEMPERO.md` file is enough. These are the places to look:
- **Shape:** the geometry of the logo or symbol, which can work as a field, a mask, a crop or a slice.
- **Material:** the textures in its deck or photos, such as xerox, scan, paper, metal or grain.
- **Tools:** anything the brand built that makes images, such as a shader, generator or engine. Its output can become the effect.
- **Type gesture:** how its fonts sit and move.
- **Colour:** used as surfaces to stand on, not only as accents.
- **Voice and rhythm:** how it speaks, and its tempo. A music brand has a BPM.

Look in the cheapest sources first: the brand guideline (Visant MCP `brand-guidelines-get`), then the deck and board, then the brand's tools and site, then the client folder. If something is missing, that is a question for the owner. Do not invent it.

## Dose it

The dose depends on the piece.

| Piece | Dose |
|---|---|
| Brand moments: showreel, campaign, hero, logo reveal, launch post | **Full.** The tempero leads. |
| Product screens people use daily: lists, editors, dashboards | **A pinch.** One live signal or one detail. Clarity comes first. |
| Money, permission, data, deletion, settings | **Almost none.** Personality shows only in tone. |
| Charts and data | **Light.** The brand's colours and type, but only while they stay readable. |

## When it is not working

- **Swap test.** For a brand moment, imagine a competitor's logo on the piece. If the piece would still fit them, it probably needs more of this brand. This test does not apply to work screens.
- **Generic defaults.** Dot grids, mesh gradients, stock noise, light leaks, ray wipes and invented construction grids are signs the piece reached for a default. Sometimes a default is still the right call; make that choice on purpose.
- **Thin material.** If the brand has little material, recutting its own plates usually beats inventing something new. A new effect is fine when it clearly grows out of the ingredients and the owner agrees.

## Example

A techno collective's showreel went through two versions.
- **v1** was clean and generic.
- **v2** was approved on first watch. Its transitions were sliced from the collective's own shader tool, and its texture came from the xerox slides of its deck. The ink tremor ran over its symbol. The cuts fell on the eighth notes of a track from its own label.
