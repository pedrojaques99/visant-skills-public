# The Visant voice, line by line

Rules are in English; every example, forbidden word and before/after sample stays
in Brazilian Portuguese, because that is the language the copy is written in.

Index:
1. Vocabulary and anglicisms
2. Rhythm and punctuation
3. Stance: blame, humor, swearing, money
4. How each block is written (hero, mechanism, proof, sample, FAQ, CTA)
5. The tics, with the fix for each
6. Before/after table of real fixes
7. Delivery checklist

## 1. Vocabulary and anglicisms

**Use:**

| instead of | write |
|---|---|
| para | pra |
| nós / nossa equipe | a gente |
| está | tá (in body copy, not in an SEO title) |
| utilizar, realizar | usar, fazer |
| aprimorar, otimizar (in sales copy) | melhorar, resolver |
| solução | ferramenta, o arquivo, o método, o livro |
| conteúdo | live, aula, capítulo, e-book (the name of the thing) |
| investimento | preço, R$ 47 |
| adquirir | comprar |
| jornada, experiência | what the person does: ler, aplicar, entregar |

**Colloquialism is a medium dose.** "pra", "a gente", "tá" go in. "cê", "mano",
"tá ligado", "sacou" stay out: they are story-speak, age fast and come unglued
from written text.

**Anglicisms only when the trade really uses them:** branding, mockup, layout,
live, vibe coding, brief, design system. **Out:** insight, delivery, mindset,
framework, pipeline, discovery, growth. The test: if a designer says it in a
client meeting without sounding like a consultant, it goes in.

**Not in the Visant voice:** revolucionário, transformador, desbloquear,
potencial máximo, eleve seu, nunca foi tão, no mundo de hoje, mercado cada vez
mais competitivo, game changer, descubra o poder/segredo, vamos mergulhar,
prepare-se para, imagine que, e se eu te dissesse.

**First person singular ("eu") is restricted to bio, author section and
behind-the-scenes.** In landing body copy and the mechanism, always "a gente".
Visant has two founders, and text that speaks for the house speaks in plural.

## 2. Rhythm and punctuation

- Average 12 words per sentence. A 30-word sentence needs a reason.
- Alternate long and short. **A verbless fragment is allowed and wanted as a
  punch**, after a sentence that carried weight, one or two per section:
  "Você passa semanas numa identidade linda, o cliente aprova, e a marca se
  comporta de outro jeito três meses depois. Toda vez."
- **Dash forbidden.** Where the hand wants a dash: period, or comma if the
  aside is short, or colon if it is a definition, or parentheses if it is a side
  note.
- **Semicolon forbidden.** In sales copy it stalls reading. It becomes a period.
- **Emoji forbidden.** `©`, `®`, `™` are trademarks and stay.
- **Separator dot (`·`) forbidden**, including metadata lists, CTA with price,
  phase labels and the social card. Conversion: a list becomes period and comma
  (`E-book de 34 páginas. 9 etapas, 1 case inteiro, leitura de uma tarde.`), a
  label becomes a colon (`Fase 1: A Estratégia`), a CTA becomes a preposition
  (`Quero o e-book por R$ 47`). Same for `|` and `•`: a graphic separator in the
  middle of a sentence is a crutch for not choosing the punctuation.
- **Anaphoric repetition is a strong device, once per page.** Saved for the
  mechanism block, where the chaining IS the argument: "A Mensagem alimenta os
  Pilares. Os Pilares filtram a Pesquisa. A Pesquisa aprofunda a Persona." Used
  twice on the same page it becomes a nursery rhyme.
- Zero exclamation marks, except a rare closing CTA.
- Section headlines without a final period. A closing sentence, with a period.

## 3. Stance

**Blame starts with the author.** The text confesses before it charges. "A gente
começava pelo logo também. Perdeu semanas assim, até inverter a ordem." That buys
the right to point out the reader's mistake afterwards. A dry accusation up front
("você começa pelo logo porque é a parte divertida") sounds arrogant without the
confession first.

**Humor is dry mockery of the market.** It targets the practice, the 40-hour
course, the expensive software, the pretty portfolio that does not bill. Never
the reader, never signposted ("brincadeira", "rs"). "Sem aula de 40 horas
ensinando a centralizar retângulo."

**Mild swearing, at most one per page**, and only where the argument hurts:
"porcaria", "cagada", "hobby caro". Never in the main headline, never in a CTA,
never in a guarantee FAQ.

**Money is a consequence of the craft.** The focus is the work done well, and
the money shows up as a result. "Processo no lugar certo cobra mais caro. A
margem vem depois, sozinha." Revenue numbers live in the proof block, not spread
across the body.

**The page disqualifies no one.** No "se você é X, nem entra". The filter works
by signaling the effort the thing requires ("o que ele exige é que você queira
trabalhar com processo em vez de só feeling"), and the FAQ settles the fit.

## 4. How each block is written

**Hero.** The eyebrow names the category (`E-book, a Metodologia Visant`). The
title is the **consequence of the mistake**, never a promise: "Se o projeto
começa pelo logo, ele volta pra revisão." The lead names the mistake and what the
product does about it, in two sentences. The trigger under the CTA carries zero
risk: "Acesso na hora, leitura no navegador. 7 dias de garantia."

**Mechanism.** The causal chain with a repeated subject (the page's anaphora).
The repetition shows the chaining without having to say "chained".

**Proof.** Raw number plus a two-word label. `R$450k / faturados`. The note below
defuses exaggeration: "A gente não vende promessa." Never illustrate a number
with an adjective.

**Sample.** Deliver the whole thing and say you did: "É a primeira das nove, do
jeito que está no livro." Then invite judgment: "Lê e julga o método antes de
pagar por ele."

**FAQ.** The question is the literal objection, in the reader's voice, with the
fear inside. The answer opens by answering and only then explains.

**CTA: desire in first person.** "Quero o e-book por R$ 47", "Quero ser um
Visionário®". Price attached to the button whenever price is the objection. Never
"Saiba mais", never "Clique aqui".

**Expansion / cross-sell.** Position the current product as a piece of a whole
without devaluing what the person is buying: "Este livro é uma peça do Visant
Club."

## 5. The tics and the fix

### Dash
`Metodologia Visant — 9 etapas` → `Metodologia Visant, 9 etapas`

### "Não é X, é Y"
The most common and the easiest to kill: **delete the negative half.**

- `Não é uma lista de etapas. É uma cascata.` → `Cada etapa alimenta a próxima.`
- `Não é um teaser. É a primeira das nove.` → `É a primeira das nove, inteira.`
- `Não é slogan. Slogan é pra fora.` → `Slogan é pra fora, pro público.`

When the contrast really is the argument, swap negation for **consequence**:
`Não é falta de talento, é falta de ordem` → `Talento você já tem. O que falta é
ordem.`

### "É sobre X"
`Branding é sobre consistência.` → `Branding faz a marca se comportar igual em
todo ponto de contato.` The question that solves it: which verb? Who does what?

### "Não é apenas X, é X e Y"
`Não é apenas um e-book, é um método completo.` → `São as 9 etapas com um case
atravessando todas.`

### Negation dangling at the end
`Feito pra ler, não pra arquivar.` → `Cada página foi desenhada.`
Rule: if the sentence ends in `, não ...`, delete from the comma on and read it
aloud. If it lost necessary information, that becomes its own affirmative
sentence.

### Repeated credibility seal
The hardest tic to see, because each occurrence alone looks fine. The reader sees
them all together. A screenshot of "trajetória real / projeto real / técnica real
/ processo real" in the same piece circulated as an example of AI text.

Limit: **one per page**, adding up `real`, `de verdade`, `na prática`, `de fato`,
`genuíno`, `autêntico`, `honesto`. The linter counts per piece (see SKILL.md).

| before | after |
|---|---|
| Sete anos de projeto real. | Sete anos entregando marca. |
| Projeto real, do zero, na sua frente. | Projeto do zero, na sua frente. |
| Os primeiros pagam menos, de verdade. | Os primeiros pagam menos. |
| Se tem uma coisa que eu aprendi na prática… | Se tem uma coisa que eu aprendi… |
| pilar bom é aquele que a marca cumpre na prática | pilar bom é aquele que a marca cumpre |

The cut almost never costs information: the seal was there defending the sentence
from a distrust the reader did not have yet. When credibility really needs to
show up, it comes as a number (`160+ lives`, `R$450k`) or a scene (`Um post fala
uma coisa, o atendimento fala outra`).

### Guru openings and cliché urgency
`Imagine que você acabou de fechar um cliente…` → start with the scene or the fact.
`Últimas vagas, corre!` → `Este lote tem 30 vagas. A cota sobe a cada compra.`

Pattern that jumps out of the table: **the after is always shorter.** If the
rewrite got longer, it probably swapped one tic for another.

The three BOXY rows are the same fix seen from three angles: state became task.
None of them had a linter tic, which is why they are here.

## 7. Delivery checklist

- [ ] `copy-lint` passes clean
- [ ] The fold names the reader's task, not their state
- [ ] No echo of the old sentence left (social card, `<title>`, description, `alt`)
- [ ] Only the tics were applied; the rest went as a proposal table
- [ ] No sentence over 30 words without a reason
- [ ] Every number checked against the source of truth
- [ ] Plan terms match the glossary (Visionário® / Visantista), in Visant's own copy
- [ ] CTA in first-person desire, with price if price is the objection
- [ ] FAQ in the reader's voice, answer opening with the answer
- [ ] At least one place admits a limit, and the blame starts with the author
- [ ] Read aloud without stumbling. If the tongue trips, rewrite.
