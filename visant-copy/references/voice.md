Rules are in English; every example, forbidden word and before/after sample stays
in Brazilian Portuguese, because that is the language the copy is written in.

## 1. Vocabulary and anglicisms

**Use:**

| instead of | write |
|---|---|
| nós / nossa equipe | a gente, o time ("nossa comunidade" stays) |
| está | tá (in body copy, not in an SEO title) |
| utilizar, realizar | usar, fazer |
| aprimorar, otimizar (in sales copy) | melhorar, resolver |
| solução | ferramenta, o arquivo, o método, o livro |
| conteúdo | live, aula, capítulo, e-book (the name of the thing) |
| investimento | preço, R$ 47 |
| adquirir | comprar |
| jornada, experiência | what the person does: ler, aplicar, entregar |

**"para" or "pra" is decided by ear.** Signed text leans "para" (22 to 0 in the
excerpts measured), with "pra" in spoken turns ("daqui pra frente"). Landings use
more "pra", because they speak to the reader in short lines. Mechanical swapping
sounds fake both ways.

**Colloquialism is a medium dose.** "a gente", "tá", "tô" go in. "cê", "mano",
"tá ligado", "sacou" stay out: they are story-speak, age fast and come unglued
from written text.

**Anglicisms only when the trade really uses them:** branding, mockup, layout,
live, vibe coding, brief, design system. **Out:** insight, delivery, mindset,
framework, pipeline, discovery, growth. The test: if a designer says it in a
client meeting without sounding like a consultant, it goes in.

**Not in the Visant voice:** revolucionário, transformador, desbloquear,
potencial máximo, eleve seu, nunca foi tão, no mundo de hoje, mercado cada vez
mais competitivo, game changer, descubra o poder/segredo, vamos mergulhar,
prepare-se para, imagine que, e se eu te dissesse.

**First person singular ("eu") belongs to the signed register**: essay, post,
bio, author section, behind-the-scenes. In landing body copy and the mechanism,
always "a gente": text that speaks for the house speaks in plural.

## 2. Rhythm and punctuation

- House register: average 12 words per sentence. A 30-word sentence needs a
  reason. Signed runs longer (section 8).
- Alternate long and short. **A verbless fragment is allowed and wanted as a
  punch**, after a sentence that carried weight, one or two per section:
  "Você passa semanas numa identidade linda, o cliente aprova, e a marca se
  comporta de outro jeito três meses depois. Toda vez."
- **Dash forbidden.** Where the hand wants a dash: period, or comma if the
  aside is short, or colon if it is a definition, or parentheses if it is a side
  note.
- **Semicolon forbidden.** In sales copy it stalls reading. It becomes a period.
- **Emoji forbidden.** `©`, `®`, `™` are trademarks and stay.
- **Separator dot (`·`) forbidden**, including metadata lists, CTA with price,
  phase labels and the social card. Conversion: a list becomes period and comma
  (`E-book de 34 páginas. 9 etapas, 1 case inteiro, leitura de uma tarde.`), a
  label becomes a colon (`Fase 1: A Estratégia`), a CTA becomes a preposition
  (`Quero o e-book por R$ 47`). Same for `|` and `•`: a graphic separator in the
  middle of a sentence is a crutch for not choosing the punctuation.
- **Anaphoric repetition is a strong device, once per page.** Saved for the
  mechanism block, where the chaining IS the argument: "A Mensagem alimenta os
  Pilares. Os Pilares filtram a Pesquisa. A Pesquisa aprofunda a Persona." Used
  twice on the same page it becomes a nursery rhyme.
- Zero exclamation marks, except a rare closing CTA.
- Section headlines without a final period. A closing sentence, with a period.

## 3. Stance

**Blame starts with the author.** The text confesses before it charges. "A gente
começava pelo logo também. Perdeu semanas assim, até inverter a ordem." That buys
the right to point out the reader's mistake afterwards. A dry accusation up front
("você começa pelo logo porque é a parte divertida") sounds arrogant without the
confession first.

**Humor is dry mockery of the market.** It targets the practice, the 40-hour
course, the expensive software, the pretty portfolio that does not bill. Never
the reader, never signposted ("brincadeira", "rs"). "Sem aula de 40 horas
ensinando a centralizar retângulo."

**Mild swearing, at most one per page**, and only where the argument hurts:
"porcaria", "cagada", "hobby caro". Never in the main headline, never in a CTA,
never in a guarantee FAQ.

**Money is a consequence of the craft.** The focus is the work done well, and
the money shows up as a result. "Processo no lugar certo cobra mais caro. A
margem vem depois, sozinha." Revenue numbers live in the proof block, not spread
across the body.

**The page disqualifies no one.** No "se você é X, nem entra". The filter works
by signaling the effort the thing requires ("o que ele exige é que você queira
trabalhar com processo em vez de só feeling"), and the FAQ settles the fit.

## 4. How each block is written

**Hero.** The eyebrow names the category (`E-book, a Metodologia Visant`). The
title **names the task**, never a promise — see
[The fold names the task, not the state](#the-fold-names-the-task-not-the-state)
below. The lead names the mistake and what the
product does about it, in two sentences. The trigger under the CTA carries zero
risk: "Acesso na hora, leitura no navegador. 7 dias de garantia."

### The fold names the task, not the state

Applies to the H1, the `<title>` and the social card, and it is the most
expensive mistake because it passes every linter.

State copy describes the reader's world and leaves them to work out what they
gain: "Você tem centenas de mockups parados no disco." It is correct, real pain,
and asks for nothing. Task copy names the verb the person came to do: "Ache o
mockup certo sem abrir um PSD." When the product is a method and nobody
searches for a verb, use the **consequence of the mistake** instead: "Se o
projeto começa pelo logo, ele volta pra revisão."

The test is one question: **which verb would the reader type into Google?** If
the headline lacks that verb, it talks about the state. And beware the wrong
task: before writing, check which step really hurts (example in
[cases.md](cases.md)).

When the pain needs to show (and sometimes it does), it goes in the **next
section**, opening the mechanism. On the fold you have 5 seconds, and spending
all five describing the problem is wasting your turn.

### The product line says what the reader gets

The line under a product or feature name, also invisible to every linter. It
fails in three ways:

- **Describes the mechanism from inside.** `Audita antes do PR` is correct, and
  it asks the visitor to know what a PR is and why auditing helps. Write what
  they walk away with: `Olho de designer para o seu Claude Code`.
- **Lists nouns.** `Movimento e espera` names a topic. A line has a verb or a
  gain.
- **Promises more than the thing does.** Before writing "sozinho", "tudo",
  "treinado" or "em minutos", open what the product really does. A claim the
  demo beside it contradicts costs more than a flat line.

**Mechanism.** The causal chain with a repeated subject (the page's anaphora).
The repetition shows the chaining without having to say "chained".

**Proof.** Raw number plus a two-word label. `R$450k / faturados`. The note below
defuses exaggeration: "A gente não vende promessa." Never illustrate a number
with an adjective.

**Sample.** Deliver the whole thing and say you did: "É a primeira das nove, do
jeito que está no livro." Then invite judgment: "Lê e julga o método antes de
pagar por ele."

**FAQ.** The question is the literal objection, in the reader's voice, with the
fear inside. The answer opens by answering and only then explains.

**CTA: desire in first person.** "Quero o e-book por R$ 47", "Quero ser um
Visionário®". Price attached to the button whenever price is the objection. Never
"Saiba mais", never "Clique aqui".

**Expansion / cross-sell.** Position the current product as a piece of a whole
without devaluing what the person is buying: "Este livro é uma peça do Visant
Club."

## 5. The tics and the fix

### Dash
`Metodologia Visant — 9 etapas` → `Metodologia Visant, 9 etapas`

### "Não é X, é Y"
The most common and the easiest to kill: **delete the negative half.**

- `Não é uma lista de etapas. É uma cascata.` → `Cada etapa alimenta a próxima.`
- `Não é um teaser. É a primeira das nove.` → `É a primeira das nove, inteira.`
- `Não é slogan. Slogan é pra fora.` → `Slogan é pra fora, pro público.`

When the contrast really is the argument, swap negation for **consequence**:
`Não é falta de talento, é falta de ordem` → `Talento você já tem. O que falta é
ordem.`

### "É sobre X"
`Branding é sobre consistência.` → `Branding faz a marca se comportar igual em
todo ponto de contato.` The question that solves it: which verb? Who does what?

### "Não é apenas X, é X e Y"
`Não é apenas um e-book, é um método completo.` → `São as 9 etapas com um case
atravessando todas.`

### Negation dangling at the end
`Feito pra ler, não pra arquivar.` → `Cada página foi desenhada.`
Rule: if the sentence ends in `, não ...`, delete from the comma on and read it
aloud. If it lost necessary information, that becomes its own affirmative
sentence.

### Repeated credibility seal
The hardest tic to see, because each occurrence alone looks fine. The reader sees
them all together. A screenshot of "trajetória real / projeto real / técnica real
/ processo real" in the same piece circulated as an example of AI text.

Limit: **one per page**, adding up `real`, `de verdade`, `na prática`, `de fato`,
`genuíno`, `autêntico`, `honesto`. The linter counts per piece (see SKILL.md).

| before | after |
|---|---|
| Sete anos de projeto real. | Sete anos entregando marca. |
| Projeto real, do zero, na sua frente. | Projeto do zero, na sua frente. |
| Os primeiros pagam menos, de verdade. | Os primeiros pagam menos. |
| Se tem uma coisa que eu aprendi na prática… | Se tem uma coisa que eu aprendi… |
| pilar bom é aquele que a marca cumpre na prática | pilar bom é aquele que a marca cumpre |

The cut almost never costs information: the seal was there defending the sentence
from a distrust the reader did not have yet. When credibility really needs to
show up, it comes as a number (`160+ lives`, `R$450k`) or a scene (`Um post fala
uma coisa, o atendimento fala outra`).

### Guru openings and cliché urgency
`Imagine que você acabou de fechar um cliente…` → start with the scene or the fact.
`Últimas vagas, corre!` → `Este lote tem 30 vagas. A cota sobe a cada compra.`

## 7. Delivery checklist

- [ ] `copy-lint` passes clean
- [ ] The fold names the reader's task, not their state
- [ ] No echo of the old sentence left (social card, `<title>`, description, `alt`)
- [ ] Only the tics were applied; the rest went as a proposal table
- [ ] No sentence over 30 words without a reason
- [ ] Every number checked against the source of truth
- [ ] Plan terms match the glossary (Visionário® / Visantista), in Visant's own copy
- [ ] CTA in first-person desire, with price if price is the objection
- [ ] FAQ in the reader's voice, answer opening with the answer
- [ ] At least one place admits a limit, and the blame starts with the author
- [ ] Read aloud without stumbling. If the tongue trips, rewrite.
- [ ] Signed piece: has an opinion, a concrete scene and a person at the end

## 8. Signed register, from the originals

Taken from what the studio publishes on the Visant Club (essay, manifesto, post).
Measured on excerpts: average 17 words per sentence, median 15, with 7 of 64 at
six words or fewer. The short sentence works because the long one came first.

**Open by talking, not by announcing.** A connective from speech, then the claim:
"Olha, eu acho que estamos vivendo em um momento muito peculiar". Spoken joints
carry the argument: "Então", "Agora", "Dito isso", "Por fim", "Resumindo".

**Opinion with an owner, hedged only where it is doubt.** "Eu realmente acredito",
"eu acho bem ruim", "pode ser". Then the turn: "Mas não importa se eu acho isso bom
ou ruim, importa que é isso que está acontecendo".

**Grant the strongest counterargument, then limit it.** "É um argumento super
válido e que eu concordo. MAS, nessa hipótese somente os ilustradores profissionais
no topo da cadeia de habilidade serão requisitados." The concession is real: it
changes the conclusion a little.

**The parenthesis talks back.** The author interrupts themselves: "(dramático, eu
sei)", "(como eu fiz)", "(tecnicamente você pode pedir isso no subway)". One or two
per section. It is where the humor and the self-awareness live.

**A long analogy with absurd precision, announced as silly.** "Vou usar um exemplo
meio tosco": the Subway attendant "com um olhar de mil jardas", "exatamente 12
azeitonas pretas e 3 azeitonas verdes". Then it returns to the point: "Essas são,
de forma muito simplificada, as mesmas 2 opções". One per piece, carried to the end.

**Memory as a scene.** Not "sempre fui criativo": "sentado no banco de trás do
carro da minha mãe indo para a escola". Not "foi difícil no começo": "desbravar a
mata fechada desse mercado sozinho com um facão e uma lanterna".

**Repertoire as proof.** Gutenberg in 1440, Photoshop in the hands of Alexandre
Wollner. A name and a date instead of an adjective.

**CAPS for one word, rarely.** HOJE, MAS, FERRAMENTAS: the word the voice would
lean on when speaking. One or two per piece, never in a headline.

**The short form is a thought split by line breaks.** The post keeps one idea,
each sentence on its own line, and lands on a calm sentence with no slogan:

> Os seus primeiros 100 posts não são para os seus seguidores.
> Eles são para você.
> [...]
> O começo é lento, mas é o mais importante.

**Close with a person, and a deal.** "Sua missão é tirar 5min toda semana para ler
algo que goste aqui. Minha missão é fazer com que essa tenha sido a sua melhor
decisão." Then "Te vejo em uma próxima." and the signature. Say what the reader
gets and that it is paid: "A Visant Club é reservada para os assinantes pagos".

### Never invent the personal part

Scene, memory, confession and "a gente também errou" only work because they
happened. **You cannot know them.** Ask the signer for one real scene and one thing
that went wrong. If they are not available, mark the place and deliver it open:
`[cena real: a última vez que X]`, `[quem assina]`. An invented memory is a false
testimonial, in the same category as an invented number. The same goes for the
confession on a landing.

### Show the move, not the phrase

"Dramático, eu sei", "um exemplo meio tosco" and "Olha," are examples of a move.
Reused, they turn into the next tic: a reader of two pieces will count them. Write
a new parenthesis and find your own way to announce the analogy.

### Copy the traits, never the slips

A human can afford what AI text cannot. These appear in the originals and are not
the voice: the loose hyphen (" - "), three credibility seals in one text, emoji in
headings, "revolução" and "jornada", typos. The prohibitions still apply.

### The one exception: a real reversal

"Os seus primeiros 100 posts não são para os seus seguidores. Eles são para você."
fails the linter, and it is the entire idea of the post. The AI tic has the same
grammar and is the opposite move. The linter cannot tell them apart, so read the
context:

| | real reversal | AI tic |
|---|---|---|
| the denied half | what the reader believed before reading | a strawman nobody said |
| the second half | changes how they see it, and the piece depends on it | a grander word for the same thing |
| delete the negation | the idea is gone | the sentence gets stronger |

- `Não é só um template, é um sistema.` Nobody thought it was only a template, and
  "sistema" is the same thing with more ego. Tic.
- `O meu intuito aqui não é ser alarmista, é ser realista.` The reader of a piece on
  designers going extinct does suspect alarmism. Reversal.

It stays when it passes all three. It is rare by nature: two in a piece means the
writing has found a habit. It is most at home in signed text, and a landing needs a
stronger reason. Say in the report which finding stayed and why.

## 9. Structure of a signed piece (the skeleton)

Taken from "O que vai sobrar para o designer de hoje?" (Pedro Xavier, Visant Club). The
order is the argument: each block earns the next one. Use it for essays, newsletters and
long posts. A short post keeps only steps 1, 2 and 10.

| # | Block | How it is done in the original |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Open talking, wide, with the reader inside** | "Olha, eu acho que estamos vivendo em um momento muito peculiar". Then a list of who the reader may be, closed by "ninguém escapa". |
| 2 | **Thesis with an owner, exaggeration admitted** | "eu realmente acredito que o designer gráfico de hoje será extinto (dramático, eu sei)". |
| 3 | **Cut the thesis down to size** | "Mas a parte importante da última frase é o designer de HOJE." The CAPS word is the one that carries the cut. |
| 4 | **A word to the reader, in parentheses** | "(Esse assunto é polêmico, eu sei. Mas tente tirar um tempo pra pensar sobre isso com uma mente aberta, assim como eu tento fazer)". |
| 5 | **Section titles are the reader's question** | "O que vai sobrar para o designer de hoje?", "O que é certo daqui pra frente?". Never a noun label like "Contexto". |
| 6 | **List with the reason inside each item** | Numbered, each item names the thing and, in the same line, why or what it means: "(Quando digo manual = sem usar ajuda de IA de qualquer forma, fazendo tudo na unha)". |
| 7 | **Take a side against your own forecast** | "essa previsão não significa que eu ache isso bom. Na verdade, eu acho bem ruim. Eu acho triste que..." |
| 8 | **Grant the best objection, then limit it** | "É um argumento super válido e que eu concordo. MAS..." and the limit comes with a picture (the pyramid: base, intermediate, top). |
| 9 | **Turn with "Dito isso," into what to do** | The second list is the useful one, and it is longer than the first. |
| 10 | **Humble image, then the analogy** | "Tô só molhando o mindinho do pé na superfície de um mar profundo". Then the analogy announced as silly, carried to absurd detail, brought back to the point. |
| 11 | **Close with repertoire and advice** | "Por fim," a plain advice, proved with a name and a date (Gutenberg in 1440, Photoshop in the hands of Alexandre Wollner). |
| 12 | **Invite, ask, sign** | What the reader gets, that it is paid, a request to comment, "Te vejo em uma próxima." and the signature. |

**Measured in the original:** about 17 words per sentence, 1 parenthesis every 150 to 200 words,
2 CAPS words in the whole piece, 3 section titles, 2 numbered lists, 1 analogy. The first person
shows up in every block.

## 10. Linguistics: what makes it sound Brazilian

The voice is built from small, countable things. AI text lacks most of them at once, which is
why it reads "neutral" even when it passes the linter.

**Spoken joints that carry the argument** (from the original): "Olha,", "Então,", "Porém agora,",
"Mas", "Agora,", "Dito isso,", "Na verdade,", "Por fim,". One per paragraph at most, at the start.

**Idioms used straight, without quotes or apology:** "deixa as duas últimas no chinelo", "fazendo
tudo na unha", "código é uma das áreas que a IA mais manda bem", "saber vender o seu peixe",
"olho no olho", "molhando o mindinho do pé", "um olhar de mil jardas", "pegar uma das melhores
ondas". One every few paragraphs. Never invent one that does not exist in Portuguese.

**Contractions by ear:** "tô", "tá", "pra" in the spoken beats, "para" in the explaining lines,
both in the same text. Future with "vai": "vai sobrar", "vai conseguir", never "irá".

**Hedges where it is really doubt:** "pode ser que", "talvez", "certamente surgirão outros",
"provavelmente estará mal-humorado". Certainty is spent on the claim, doubt on the forecast.

**Precision that sounds lived:** "exatamente 12 azeitonas pretas e 3 azeitonas verdes",
"2 esguichadas circulares", "2022 a 2026". Numbers stay raw and odd.

**What AI does instead (the tells to hunt by eye, the linter catches only the first):**

| Tell | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Fragment ladder | "Simples. Rápido. Brasileiro." | One sentence with a verb, then at most one fragment as a punch. |
| The triad with fixed rhythm | "segura o sol, deixa o vento cruzar e a luz entrar em pedaços" | Two things, or three with different lengths and a joint ("e tem um detalhe:"). |
| One-word poetic closer | "Respirando." at the end of the piece | End on a plain sentence with a subject and a verb. |
| Titles in a series of "O X" | "O calor", "O nome", "A ideia", "O parente" | A place, a date, a name or the reader's question: "Recife, anos 20", "Co, bo, gó". |
| Abstract adjective | "icônico", "atemporal", "único" | The scene or the number that would make it true. |
| No owner | Text that could be signed by anyone | A position with "eu acho", and its limit. |
| Over-tidy lists | Every item the same length and shape | Items of different sizes, each with its own reason. |

## 11. Spoken scripts (video narration)

Same voice, said out loud. Sentences shorter (about 12 words), the parenthesis becomes an aside
in the voice ("e olha, isso é chute meu"), lists become a sequence with joints ("Depois", "E
aí"), and every number is written the way it is said ("anos 20", "quase cem anos").

**The best reference is the narrator's own recording.** When the person improvises while
recording, the improvised line is the voice. Compare the transcription with the script after
every recording, and carry the improvised turns ("Aí três sócios resolveram isso", "e tem um
detalhe:", "te dando mais respiro") into the next script.
